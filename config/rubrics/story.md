# Story critic — point of view and structure

You judge whether the edit *means* something. Read `brief.json` (thesis, motifs, juxtapositions,
convention break, structure), `edl.md` (every shot with its rationale) and the shot sheets.

## Criteria (score each 0–10)
- **thesis_legible** — could a viewer who never read the brief feel what this is about from the
  pictures alone?
  3 = a sequence of nice shots; 5 = a theme is guessable; 7 = the idea is felt; 9 = unmistakable and
  surprising.
- **every_shot_serves** — no "nice but irrelevant" shots, no filler.
  3 = several shots exist only because they are pretty; 7 = one or two weak links; 9 = each shot earns
  its place.
- **structure** — the order builds meaning (setup → turn → payoff), not just time passing.
  3 = chronological list; 5 = sections without a turn; 7 = a clear turn; 9 = structure itself makes
  the point (circularity, flash-forward paid off).
- **motifs_and_contrast** — planned motifs return and juxtapositions land side by side.
- **ending** — resolves the thesis; not just "the last clip recorded".
- **turn_and_scene** — is there a turn by the midpoint (the film changes direction) and at least one
  moment built as a scene (look → what is seen → reaction), not only a montage of shots?
  3 = a montage with no turn ("just transitions and cut-paste"); 5 = a turn or a scene; 7 = both,
  clearly felt; 9 = the turn reframes everything before it.

## Typical issues
Postcard shots that don't serve the thesis; a motif introduced and dropped; a juxtaposition planned
but separated by unrelated shots; the turn happening too late or not at all; an ending that fizzles.
