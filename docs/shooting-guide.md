# Shooting guide — footage that lets the edit go past 6/10

The edit can only be as good as the moments it's given. These are the things the Sydney and
Wollongong footage didn't have, in order of how much they'd help.

## 1. Light
- Shoot the hero places at **golden hour** (the hour after sunrise / before sunset) or blue hour.
  Overcast flattens colour and no grade fully brings it back.
- On a grey day, go close (textures, water, rock, faces) instead of wide skies.

## 2. Stable, slow, long
- Use the Pocket 3's gimbal (Follow mode), walk slowly, heel-to-toe. Hold each shot **8–15 s**; start
  recording 2 s before the action and stop 2 s after.
- Slow motion: **4K 60 fps** for water, birds, hair, spray (120 fps at 1080p for extreme slow-mo).

## 3. Cover every place like an editor needs it
For each place, four shots: **wide** (where are we), **medium**, **close detail** (texture, hands,
water), **one moving shot** (push in, reveal from behind something, slow orbit). That gives match cuts
and fast montages real variety instead of eight versions of the same angle.

## 4. Film the peaks more than once
Waves bursting, birds lifting, a jump, a splash: film 3–5 of each. The edit times these peaks onto
the music's beats; more takes = a better peak on the right beat.

## 5. Transitions in the camera
- **Whip pan:** end a shot with a fast pan and start the next one with a fast pan in the **same
  direction**.
- **Mask:** walk past a pole, tree or wall so it fills the frame at the end of one shot and the start
  of the next.
- **Sky tilt:** tilt up to the sky at the end of a place and start the next place from the sky.

## 6. You, as the story
Put yourself in 3–5 key moments, from behind or the side: walking in, reaching the edge, looking out,
sitting, leaving. A tripod or a friend helps. That small arc (arrive → see → reflect) is what turns
nice shots into a story.

## 7. Sound
- Record **20–30 s of clean natural sound** at every place (waves, wind, footsteps, birds) with
  nobody talking. It becomes the sound design.
- Don't talk during shots you might use (a stray voice can't be cut out of the sea sound).

## 8. Avoid
- Shooting from inside a car or through a windshield.
- People you don't want in the film: frame them out while shooting; it can't be fixed later.
- Tiny jittery handheld clips under 3 s.

## 9. For Instagram
Keep the subject in the middle of the frame (it can be reframed to vertical 9:16 later), or shoot the
hero moments twice: once horizontal, once vertical.

## 10. Bring an idea
Before the trip, one line for what the day is about ("chasing the perfect wave", "first light to last
light", "the sea is breathing"). Shooting toward an idea gives the edit a premise and a payoff, and
those are what the 9/10 intro and outro had that the films didn't.
